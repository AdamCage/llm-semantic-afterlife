# Scientific reading checklist

## Does the conclusion follow?

Read the source, not just the citation. Trace numbers to a table row/condition,
and prose to the comparison actually estimated. Read final corrections and
review decisions; old hypotheses and superseded figures can survive in drafts.
If evidence is missing, say so instead of filling the gap from plausible prose.

For each central claim ask:

- What is the population and what sample was actually observed?
- Is the analysis conditional on completion/survival/selection?
- What is the independent replicate unit? Are correlated observations treated
  as independent or unlike model families pooled without justification?
- Does the measured contrast establish a sign, equivalence, causality, a
  mechanism or only association? A CI crossing zero establishes none of those.
- Are missing or failed cells omitted, shown as missing, or imputed as zero?
- Are finite horizons, censoring and competing failure modes named correctly?
- Are time origin, units, normalisation and protocol stated where necessary?
- Is a display projection being used for an inference that needs original data?
- Is a qualitative illustration masquerading as an empirical trajectory?
- Does the practical advice describe an intervention actually tested here?

Do not import the source study's peculiar limitations into every future study.
For example, repeated prompting versus native attention matters for the original
Semantic Afterlife film; another study may instead require a sampling-bias,
measurement-noise, clinical-population or causal-identification qualification.

## Narrative fidelity

A popular explanation may remove algebra, but must preserve the experimental
distinction that supports the conclusion. Check whether the opening promise
matches the measured outcome. Do not sell a study of one protocol as a universal
statement about a technology. Do not equate an intuitive everyday word with a
weaker operational metric without explaining the gap.

Negative outcomes deserve the same precise wording as positive ones. An
unidentified estimate, nonsignificant contrast, failed replication and supported
negative effect are different outcomes. Avoid dramatic headlines that erase
those distinctions.

For every finding, record scene ID/time, exact narration or visual, source
locator, why the inference fails and the smallest accurate revision. Review
revised spoken text and visuals together; a corrected footer cannot repair an
overstated narration.
