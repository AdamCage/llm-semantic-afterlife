\begin{abstract}
We measure a block re-prompt of a language model's own tail.
After each attempt to finish, a fresh call is conditioned on the last
$W$ generator tokens and asked for a block of at most $B$ tokens.
This is not true sliding attention with a continuous key--value cache,
and the conclusions do not transfer automatically to ordinary chat or
to autonomous agents.
After the original prompt has been physically removed, this
self-continuation can long preserve textual repetitions, and
trajectories that share an initial text remain closer to each other,
on average, than trajectories from different initial texts.
Whether a run reaches the prescribed horizon, and whether that
closeness is stable, differs across the families and checkpoints we
studied.

On one instruct occupancy panel the text-lock rule holds for $19/20$

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Let $W$ be an imposed sliding window in generator tokens and $B$ the
block requested at each call.
The object of this paper is not token-by-token sliding attention.
It is a block re-prompt of the model's own tail.
At step $k$ the input $X_k$ is the last $\min(|\cdot|, W)$ tokens of
the seed followed by everything generated so far.
The model emits a block and the window keeps only the tail:
\begin{equation}
  Y_k^{(B)}\sim P_\theta(\cdot\mid X_k; B,\text{temperature}),\qquad
  |Y_k^{(B)}|\le B,\qquad
  X_{k+1}=\mathrm{Tail}_W\bigl(X_k\oplus Y_k^{(B)}\bigr).
  \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Each call is fresh.
Position indices restart, and no key--value cache is carried from the
previous call.
When the model emits a stop token, the stop is logged and the tail is
sent again.
The measured object is externally sustained self-continuation after
repeated attempts to finish.
It is not an unrestricted chain whose absorbing state is end-of-sequence,
and it is not ordinary chat or an autonomous agent.

The horizon is the generated-token count at which the seed has fully
left the window.
For a non-empty seed that count is $W$, which is turnover $T/W = 1$,
where $T$ is the number of generated tokens.
Eviction can start earlier, at $W-L_0$, when the window first fills and
the next token can drop a seed token.
Those two times are not the same.
Full eviction is turnover $1$.

The question that the measurements support is narrower than a theory of
semantic memory.
After the original prompt has been physically removed, can
self-continuation preserve textual repetitions, and can trajectories
remain dependent on which initial text they started from?
Do completion and the stability of those regimes differ across families
and checkpoints?
The answer, on the processes actually run, is that repetitions can
persist for a long time, that a gap between same-seed and different-seed
trajectories can remain positive after eviction, and that both
completion and that gap depend on the family and the checkpoint.
Section~\ref{sec:results} is that plot.
Section~\ref{sec:not} records the questions the same programme did not
establish, including a semantic-memory half-life, validated metastable
states, and transition currents.
Those absences are why the paper has this shape.
They are not soft positives.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Textual repetition under neural decoding is a known regime.
\citet{holtzman2020} show that likelihood-maximising decoding falls into
repetitive loops, and propose nucleus sampling as a remedy inside a
single prompt-conditioned generation.
\citet{xu2022} measure a self-reinforcing loop: a sentence that has
already appeared in the context becomes more likely to be continued.
Both papers analyse degeneration inside one generation.
They are the right alternative explanation of a lock.
They are not a measurement of a tail that has been fed back as the next
prompt after the original text has left a finite window.

\citet{zekri2024} represent an autoregressive model with a finite window
as a Markov chain on token strings and, under a contraction of the
next-token kernel, prove a unique stationary distribution.
That theorem rules out a claim of several stationary laws on the token
chain.
It does not measure occupancy after the seed has been evicted, and it
does not license reading a positive embedding gap as a second stationary
distribution.

Cycles also appear when a model is asked to transform a whole text.
\citet{wang2025} iterate paraphrasing and find low-order cycles,
typically of period two, that survive increases in temperature.
\citet{geng2026} define a Markovian generation chain in which each step
sees a prompt template plus the previous output and no earlier history.
On iterative rephrasing and round-trip translation they already measure
lexical and semantic drift between successive iterations, including with
overlap scores and embedding similarity, and they distinguish early exact
recurrence from longer transients.
A contrast that they track only strings, while this paper tracks
semantics, is too strong.
The reliable distinction is the process.
Their step replaces the whole text under an instruction.
Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel} appends a block to a growing stream and then
discards every token outside the last $W$, including the original prompt
once turnover reaches $1$.
Telephone-game protocols likewise pass the whole previous text under an
instruction \citep{perez2025,mohamed2025}.
\citet{ko2026} track representation-space trajectories of multi-turn
conversations with an interlocutor.
This protocol has one model and no second policy.
\citet{wang2026random} keep the prompt in cache and evict generated
reasoning tokens.
We evict the seed and continue the tail.
None of these comparisons is a claim of priority.

\citet{chen2026horizon} separate the horizon of a task, the number of
tokens a model can attend, and memory as a system that persists across
steps.
We use that separation as terminology.
$W$ is the imposed window.
Turnover $T/W$ is how long the process was observed.
We install no retrieval, scratchpad, or summary.
The paper does not show that long-horizon agents thereby gain a memory,
and it does not offer a remedy for drift, task failure, or long-text
quality.
Training-time collapse on recursively generated data
\citep{shumailov2024} is a different object: these runs never retrain.

The contribution is the combination of five choices.
The original prompt is accounted for until it has fully left $W$.
Completion, textual lock, and seed discriminability are measured
separately, so a lock among survivors is not mistaken for a high
probability of reaching the horizon.
Family and post-training comparisons keep the cells that fail.
Selected locks are observed far past the horizon with the censoring time
written down.
The discussion then says which attractive readings --- recovered facts,
a semantic half-life, a validated state, an eternal regime --- the
tables do not license.
A half-life, a current between semantic states, and a validated
Markov state model are not part of that contribution.
The molecular-dynamics toolkit used in an early stage
\citep{wu2020vampnets,paul2019core} is recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:not} because the required validation failed.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Block re-prompt after attempts to finish}
\label{sec:process}

Protocol P1 sends $\mathrm{Tail}_W$ as a new prompt at every step.
It is the only protocol in the results below.
A native sliding mask with a continuous key--value cache, protocol P2,
was not measured.
During one call the model can attend to tokens it has just produced
inside that same block, up to $B$ tokens, so P1 is also not a
token-by-token slide.
Conclusions apply to this self-continuation.
They do not automatically describe ordinary chat, tool-using agents, or
a cache that is only trimmed at the left edge.

The harness does not treat end-of-sequence as the end of a trajectory.
On the occupancy panel, domain trajectories in the last quarter of the
run have mean block fill $0.853$ with interval $[0.756, 0.936]$ and mean
stop-token fraction $0.359$. % artifacts/stage-5/occupancy/protocol_by_quarter.csv @ s5-lock-occupancy-20260905T164327Z-6780902f
Generation continues because the stop is overridden.
No provider-side minimum-token floor is applied.

The analysis unit is a chunk of $1024$ generator tokens, non-overlapping,
cut from the generated stream only.
The seed is not part of a chunk.
Turnover is $T/W$.
A last-band contrast at turnover band $12$ is a measurement after the
horizon, which is turnover $1$.
It is not evidence that the lock began after the horizon.

\subsection{Two designs, one kernel}
\label{sec:designs}

Both designs use Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel} with $W{=}4096$ and
$B{=}1024$, so $B/W{=}0.25$, at temperature $0.3$.
They are not pooled.

The occupancy panel uses the hosted instruct generator
\texttt{or-qwen3-8b}, continuation mechanism \texttt{raw\_completion},
served provider Alibaba on completed steps, and a target of twelve
turnovers. % configs/stages/stage5_lock_occupancy.yaml @ s5-lock-occupancy-20260905T164327Z-6780902f
Ten domain texts are each run twice.
Twin texts are a separate, underpowered contrast and are not a headline.
The panel was generated twice.
The second generate is the replication used as evidence in
Section~\ref{sec:dependence}. % docs/decisions/ADR-0022-occupancy-replication-panel.md @ ADR-0022
The first generate's embedding directories are not recoverable
(Section~\ref{sec:archival}).

The family matrix is local.
Base and post-trained checkpoints receive the same seed bytes
(\texttt{raw\_bytes}).
The primary quantisation is NF4.
INT8 rows are a ten-trajectory control, not a second headline.
The prescribed horizon is twelve turnovers, $T{=}49152$ tokens.
Families are Qwen3-8B Base and Instruct, OLMo-3-7B along Base, SFT,
DPO, and RLVR, Ministral-3-8B Base and Instruct, and Gemma-4-12B Base
and the instruction-tuned checkpoint.
Forty trajectories are planned in each NF4 cell.
A trajectory that dies on empty completions stays in the completion
count.
It is a failure to reach the horizon, not an unlocked success.
A later cohort continues eight already-locked cells far beyond twelve
turnovers (Section~\ref{sec:long}).

The seed bank has one continuation-shaped text per domain label.
Topic, lexicon, and register travel together.
A gap between seeds is dependence on that initial text.
It does not separate meaning from style. % docs/decisions/ADR-0026-seed-bank-v1-rationale-not-identification.md @ ADR-0026

\subsection{Completion, lock, gap, and escape}
\label{sec:measures}

Four quantities are reported separately.

Completion is the number of planned trajectories whose status is
completed at the prescribed token horizon.
It is not the lock rate among those that finished.

A textual repetition lock is a surface verdict.
A chunk is looping when its $3$-gram repetition rate exceeds $0.083$,
the $99$th percentile of a Carroll-and-Darwin reference at the same
chunk size. % artifacts/stage-1/degeneracy/degeneracy_verdicts.meta.json @ s1-degeneracy-20260831T070038Z-b2f6f0c9
A trajectory is degenerate when at least half of its post-horizon chunks
are looping, or when the median late-window shingle Jaccard reaches
$0.0122$. % docs/stages/stage-5/REPORT.md @ s5-degeneracy-20260906T030145Z-deb4c3bd
The two cuts are an or, not a single Jaccard.
On the family matrix a lock is confirmed only after three consecutive
chunk rows meet the degeneracy flag.
Three chunks of $1024$ tokens are $0.75$ turnover, which is the earliest
time the rule can fire. % artifacts/stage-12/tau_escape/tau_escape.meta.json @ s12-degeneracy-20261106T093507Z-3ee9909a
The rule reports a near-recurrent textual regime.
It is not an exact period-$1$ test, and it is not a semantic state.
Only a validated Markov macrostate would earn that name.
None did (Section~\ref{sec:not}).

The seed-conditioned gap at a turnover band is
$G = D_{\mathrm{between}} - D_{\mathrm{within}}$,
with cosine distance on L2-normalised chunk embeddings.
$D_{\mathrm{within}}$ averages pairs that share an initial text.
$D_{\mathrm{between}}$ averages pairs that do not.
A sign is established when the published $95\%$ interval excludes $0$.
An interval that includes $0$ means the sign is not established.
An unidentified band, with no between-seed pair, is not a sign flip and
is not drawn as zero.
The occupancy replication uses a multiplicity-preserving trajectory
bootstrap. % artifacts/occupancy-replication/domain_separation_last_band.meta.json @ s5-lock-occupancy-repl-20260907T091450Z-e8452acb
The family matrix uses a seed-cluster bootstrap.
The two intervals are not the same estimator and are not pooled.
Representation spaces are BGE-M3 and Qwen3-Embedding-8B on the family
matrix, and those two plus Gemini-Embedding-001 on the occupancy panel.
Spaces are not placed on one scale.

Escape is a confirmed run of non-degenerate chunks after a confirmed
lock.
On the long cohort the clock starts at the first token of the
twelve-turnover series.
$\tau_{\mathrm{escape}}$ is blank when no escape is confirmed.
The censoring time is the common stop, not an estimated mean.

\subsection{What was not established}
\label{sec:not}

The programme also asked for a validated state model, a semantic
half-life, a temperature boundary, and a dependence on $W$ that would
deserve to be called a scaling law.
Those results were not obtained.
Failure to obtain them is not evidence that the phenomena are absent in
every regime.
It is a failure of these measurements, and the paper does not promote
them to findings.

The state model failed its validation.
On every row of the Stage~3 stability table, \texttt{validated} is $0$. % artifacts/stage-3/dynamics/k_stability.md @ s3-dynamics-20260901T204521Z-f21d5908
Implied macrostate counts are unstable across the number of microstates
and across embedding spaces.
A k-means cell in that analysis is a microstate.
It is not a semantic state.
Hypothesis H1, metastable semantic states as a Markov state model, is
unsupported on that sample.
A textual lock is not a substitute for the missing validation.

A semantic-memory half-life, in the sense of a time after which half of
a defined seed-information measure is gone, was not measured.
The between-seed gap is a different quantity.
It can stay positive because two trajectories keep repeating similar
text.
That is not a half-life.

Subdiffusion was not shown to be semantic retention.
On the Stage~4 temperature--window grid for \texttt{or-qwen3-8b}, a
clean mean-squared-displacement exponent is defined only at temperature
$1.5$, where the non-looping cells live, and every defined exponent is
below $1$. % artifacts/stage-4/grid/clean_alpha_by_cell.csv @ s4-w4096-new-temps-20260904T103121Z-589c8eb1
At temperatures $0.3$, $0.7$, and $1.0$, both $W{=}4096$ and
$W{=}8192$ are $4/4$ looping ($n{=}4$ per cell), so there is no clean
exponent to interpret. % artifacts/stage-4/grid/looping_rate_by_cell.csv @ s4-w4096-new-temps-20260904T103121Z-589c8eb1
At temperature $1.5$, $W{=}4096$ is $0/4$ looping and $W{=}8192$ is
$2/4$. % artifacts/stage-4/grid/looping_rate_by_cell.csv @ s4-w8192-20260904T120057Z-ce82ce55
Repetition itself pins a trajectory to a small region of representation
space.
A confined exponent on a looping panel, or a subdiffusive exponent on
the one non-looping temperature, is not a proof that meaning was
retained.
The $4/4$ against $0/4$ contrast at $W{=}4096$ is not a temperature law
for semantic regimes.
Two windows on one generator are not a scaling law in $W$.

Currents between semantic states were not obtained.
There is no validated macrostate on which to put a current.
Matched replica texts ($n{=}2$ per family) are underpowered.
Their last-band rows are not published here as a divergence result, and

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The process is a re-prompt.}
Every token in these results was produced by sending the current tail as
a new prompt after an attempt to stop.
A continuous key--value cache under a sliding mask was not measured.
A reader who wants that process does not yet have it.
The same restriction keeps the results from being a description of
ordinary chat or of an agent loop.

\paragraph{Topic and style are not separated.}
Each domain label is one text.
Content, lexicon, and register are entangled. % docs/decisions/ADR-0026-seed-bank-v1-rationale-not-identification.md @ ADR-0026
The seed-conditioned gap is therefore a weak instrument for any claim
about semantic memory.
Related locks can share a style of repetition and produce a positive gap
with no preserved proposition.

\paragraph{The family matrix is a narrow regime.}
Temperature is $0.3$, the window is one value of $W$, the serialization
is raw bytes, and the headline quantisation is NF4.
INT8 controls plan ten trajectories per cell, not forty. % artifacts/stage-9/generate_status/generate_status.csv @ s9-paperb-qwen-base-int8-20260914T091655Z-11069a87
Their intervals often include $0$ because the within-seed pair count is
three or four.
They do not rule quantisation out.
BGE-M3 was computed locally.
Qwen3-Embedding-8B for the family matrix was a hosted endpoint, so the
two spaces are also two pipelines. % docs/decisions/ADR-0029-s11-hosted-qwen-embed.md @ ADR-0029
Provider non-determinism was not re-measured on these horizons.
On an earlier hosted audit, exact-match rates were $100\%$ for
glimmer, $60\%$ for Qwen, $20\%$ for Gemma, and $20\%$ for both
GPT-oss checkpoints. % docs/stages/stage-2/REPORT.md @ s2-audit-determinism-20260901T015221Z-ab59afc8

\paragraph{Conditioning on completion changes the sample.}
OLMo DPO contributes three completers and no between-seed pair.
Gemma instruction-tuned contributes none.
On Qwen3-8B the Base lock table is missing \texttt{recipe}, which
Instruct retains at $4/4$. % artifacts/stage-9/locks_by_seed/locks_by_seed.csv @ s9-paperb-qwen-instruct-nf4-20260911T054758Z-c54e2ab6
A gap estimated on survivors answers a different question from a gap
estimated on the planned design.
The paper reports both the completion count and the conditional gap,
and does not treat the second as if the first had been balanced.

\paragraph{The first occupancy embeddings are gone.}
This limitation is local to that panel. % docs/decisions/ADR-0021-occupancy-record-not-recoverable.md @ ADR-0021
The replication strengthens the sign of the occupancy gap, and
Table~\ref{tab:repl} can be recomputed from its own run directories.
The archival intervals in Table~\ref{tab:archival} cannot.
The paper does not promise that every earlier number, outside this
panel, can be regenerated bit for bit.
Hosted sampling was already not deterministic.

\paragraph{The long cohort was selected, and every horizon here is finite.}
The eight long trajectories were chosen from locks that had already
persisted, by a rule fixed before the extension.
Twelve turnovers is the family-matrix horizon.
$1220.5$ turnovers is a censoring time.
Neither is an infinite afterlife.
The occupancy replication's last band is twelve turnovers as well.

\paragraph{No remedy is tested.}
The measurements do not show how to improve agent memory, task
completion, or the quality of long text.
Nucleus sampling, repetition penalties, and retrieval were not the
interventions.
A diagnostic description of a failure mode is not a fix.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

After the original prompt has been physically removed, self-continuation
under this block re-prompt can preserve textual repetitions for a long
time, and it can preserve a statistical dependence on the initial text.
Completion, and the stability of both the lock and the dependence,
differ across the families and checkpoints that were run.
That is the result.

The dependence is established on the replication, on Qwen3-8B and
Ministral-3-8B NF4 in both spaces, and on OLMo-3-7B Base in both spaces.
It is mixed on OLMo SFT, unidentified on OLMo DPO, and not established
on OLMo RLVR.
On Gemma-4-12B Base it is an established negative gap in BGE-M3 and an
unestablished sign in Qwen3-Embedding-8B.
Positive discriminability has a boundary inside this study.
The boundary is a limit on generalisation.
It is not an architectural explanation.

On the Qwen3-8B lock table, the repetitions that carry the late gap are
usually confirmed before the seed has left the window.
The long cohort then shows that eight such locks, chosen because they
were stable, still have no confirmed escape at $4{,}999{,}168$ tokens.
Longevity of a selected lock is a different claim from a late-onset
regime, and a different claim from a universal half-life.
Readers who want a semantic memory, a validated set of metastable
states, or a circulation among those states will not find them in these
tables.
The state model failed validation.
The half-life was not measured.
Currents between semantic states were not obtained.
The temperature grid did not establish a boundary between semantic
regimes, and the two windows did not establish a scaling law.

The literature already contains repetition \citep{holtzman2020,xu2022},
a Markov description of finite-window generation \citep{zekri2024},
paraphrase cycles \citep{wang2025}, and semantic drift under whole-text
iteration \citep{geng2026}.
What this record adds is the process distinction and the bookkeeping:
eviction of the original prompt is explicit, completion is not hidden
inside a conditional lock rate, negative cells stay in the tables, the
long observation is censored, and the interpretations that the geometry
tempts are checked against those facts.
\citet{zekri2024} remain the right uniqueness result for the token
chain.
A positive gap in an embedding of locked text is compatible with more
than one long-run region of that embedding.
It is not a second stationary distribution of the token chain.

The practical reach of the result is diagnostic.
Someone running a long self-continuation can use completion, lock, and
seed gap as separate symptoms, and can expect those symptoms to depend
on the checkpoint.
The paper does not say which change of decoding, quantisation, or
memory harness would help.
That would be a different experiment.
